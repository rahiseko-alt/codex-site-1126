To the Medical Team at EmCell Clinic, 

My name is A.C.. I was your patient in January 2015. I am writing because I believe you deserve to know what happened after I left Kyiv. Not just my story — but both stories. Because together they carry something I believe has genuine value for every patient who walks through your doors. I did not come alone in 2015. I came with my partner. We were treated in the same week, by the same team, with the same remarkable protocol. I received five injections. He received twelve. The nurse — if I recall correctly — had to exercise considerable persuasion to get me through mine. I have never made peace with needles. We left Kyiv carrying the same gift. What happened next is where the stories diverge. I drank water. I protected the window. I treated what your team had given me as exactly what it was — something irreplaceable that demanded stewardship, not celebration. I did not drink alcohol. I did not abuse the recovery period. I treated with the respect that I felt it deserved. I let the biology do what you had set in motion. He ( my business partner, at the time) felt so remarkable so quickly that within one week he went out and drank alcohol throughout the night (closing the bar at day break). The stem cells did not survive it. The gift was gone before it had time to take root. He has since passed away from a heart attack. He was ten years younger than me. I am telling you this not to cast a shadow over his memory, which I hold with love and respect — but because that contrast carries a responsibility I feel obligated to honor. The treatment you offer is not a transaction, -Life- is not a transaction.. It is not something that works regardless of what the recipient does afterward. It is a living intervention that requires the patient to become a worthy environment for what has been given. A program is only as good as its user. The most precise biological gift in the world cannot survive a host who does not protect it. I am now 63 years old. I weigh 115 kilograms. My waist remains 36 inches. My body fat sits at 13 to 15 percent ( though sitting closer to 15%, most of the time. I train with the same intensity I brought to your clinic eleven years ago — benching over 110 kilograms for repeated sets of 10-20 repetitions, maintaining daily cardiovascular work, and operating at a physical level that most men half my age cannot/wont sustain (it’s always a choice). I eat once a day — a single meal between 2:00 and 3:00 in the afternoon — following a ketogenic nutritional philosophy that keeps my metabolism clean, my insulin sensitivity intact, and my inflammation markers low. I take no medications. I have no high blood pressure. At 63, on no prescriptions, with full insulin sensitivity and a 36-inch waist, I am aware that this profile is uncommon. I do not take it for granted, I am blessed. I have built it deliberately, and I protect it daily. In the years since leaving EmCell I spent a full year working in the jungles of Southeast Asia — humbling, demanding field conditions, mentally sharp, physically resilient, healthy throughout. I built an international development institution now operating under international charitable organizations, writing proposals and structural infrastructures that required sustained cognitive clarity and creative depth. I wrote a literary novel — a work of spiritual fiction now preparing to be placed in the hands of those who need it most — that demanded a quality of mental presence I trace directly to the clarity your protocol restored in me. I am not a man who attributes things loosely. I am rigorous about cause and effect. When I examine eleven years of sustained cognitive performance, physical integrity, and biological resilience against what I know of my body before January 2015 — the conclusion is not sentimental. It is truly meaningful, and yet clinical. Your treatment was the architectural foundation of everything that followed. I have maintained contact with your clinic over the years because I have never forgotten what happened in Kyiv. I remember the preparation. I remember the protocol. I remember the days afterward when my body fought and then surrendered and then rebuilt. I remember the weeks that followed when something had shifted — quietly, fundamentally, irreversibly. I am writing now because I am ready to come back… I am not coming back because I am in decline. I am coming back because I understand what that first treatment gave me, and I want to build on a foundation that has already proven itself over eleven extraordinary years. I want the reinforcement before the ground shifts — not after. This time I am coming with something I did not have in 2015 — a self induced, structured 90 day post-treatment protocol covering physical training, cognitive programming, nutritional architecture, and complete biological optimization. I intend to document everything. Every marker, every observation, every measurable shift. Because I believe that a rigorously documented longitudinal outcome — from a healthy, (somewhat) high-performance patient with a proven prior response — could be of genuine value to your research and to the patients who come after me, that choose to maximize this amazing gift. . If my story, and the contrast it carries, can help even one future patient understand that stewardship of this gift is not optional — that the protocol continues long after they leave Kyiv — then sharing it is not just an opportunity. It is a responsibility. I am based between Europe and the SouthEast Asia. I am prepared to travel soon, and I am prepared to commit fully. I would be honored to speak with your medical team directly. I am the same man who walked into your clinic in January 2015. Arguably stronger. Certainly wiser. And ready.

With deep respect and gratitude,
