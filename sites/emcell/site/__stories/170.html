Health update one month following the treatment
Patient: C.N.J.
Gender: Male
Age: 4 y.o.
Country: UAE
Diagnosis: Autism

Hello,

My son, C., had stem cell treatment in on 15-16 December.  We are also doing 3 months of NasalExoDrop. 

Below is C.’s progress over the last two weeks. It’s amazing the progress we’re seeing. 

1. Cognitive & Learning Skills 

Across ABA and speech, C. has shown: 

• Strong improvements in matching (objects and pictures), now up to 10 items even in messy arrays

• Progress in sorting, moving from 2 colors to 3 colors

• Better attention span, staying on task longer

• Improved receptive language, consistently following instructions

• Increased generalization readiness (roll, throw, etc.)

• More independent initiation (choosing activities, approaching tasks himself) 

These are foundational cognitive skills that support language, academics, and daily functioning. 

2. Expressive Language 

This has been one of the most exciting areas. 

Spontaneous words and phrases emerging: 

• hello, bye, yay, yeah

• done, ready, no, ok

• tickle, cuddles

• Callan (self‑name)

• turtle, dog, crocodile, apple

• circle, triangle, star

• red, yellow, purple

• three, eight

• I run

• thank you 

 Imitated but meaningful words: 

• sit, climbing, car, cat

• again, help, eat

• down, come down, get down 

What this means: 

• He’s using functional, social, conceptual, and emotional language

• He’s retrieving words across multiple categories

• He’s beginning to use phrases, not just single words

• His spontaneous vocabulary is expanding weekly

• He’s using words in context, not randomly 

This is exactly what a language expansion phase looks like. 

3. Social Engagement 

Across ABA, speech, and PT: 

• Eye contact has significantly improved

• He greets therapists with smiles

• He stays connected during tasks

• He gives high‑fives on command

• He engages in pretend play (eggs, cutting, hiding objects)

• He remains seated during breaks and chooses to continue working 

This shows emotional connection, trust, and social readiness. 

4. Motor & Sensory Development (PT + ABA) 

• Tolerating core‑strengthening exercises

• Completing balance training

• Better motor imitation (xylophone, clapping, actions)

• Improved body awareness

• Tolerating sensory attachments

• Completing full sessions without breaks

• Increased stamina — now ready for 1‑hour PT sessions 

This is a major shift in his physical regulation and endurance. 

5. HBOT Progress 

Week 1 (5–9 Jan) 

• Only 1 full session completed

• Ear redness prevented multiple sessions

• One attempt ended in distress

• Early‑phase sensitivity expected 

Week 2 (19–23 Jan)

• Completed all 5 sessions

• Stayed calm, engaged, and regulated

• Played with puzzles, watched Paw Patrol

• Spontaneously said “I run” to the nurse 

This shows full physiological and emotional adaptation to HBOT. 

6. Emotional & Behavioral Regulation 

• Initial hyperactivity but quick settling

• Minimal crying, and fast recovery

• Longer tolerance for structured tasks

• Smooth transitions into therapy rooms

• Increased flexibility and resilience

• More cooperative, more joyful, more connected 

This is one of the strongest indicators of nervous system stability. 

Overall Pattern 

Across all therapies, C.is showing coordinated progress in: 

• expressive language

• receptive understanding

• motor planning

• sensory tolerance

• emotional regulation

• social engagement

• cognitive flexibility

• endurance 

This is not random improvement — it’s a system‑wide developmental shift.
