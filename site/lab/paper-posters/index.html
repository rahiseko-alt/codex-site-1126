Paper Posters, a Lab experiment

3DNew

Every award from the season as a printed sheet on a diagonal strip. Drag or scroll and the paper catches the air, lags behind its pin and bows, then irons itself flat when you stop.

Dependencies

three.jsWebGL2 :: shaders, render targets

A stack of prints in a hurry

Carry a stack of posters across a room and they don’t move as one block. The top sheet lifts at the corners, the edges lag behind your hand, and when you stop they settle back in a slow sway. This is that, with the seventeen awards from the season. Where an award comes with a certificate, it’s the real certificate. Where it doesn’t (galleries, showcases, a newsletter), it’s a house sheet: the date blown up past the edge in the listing’s own colors.

Rebuilt as paper

The technique is Jesper Landberg’s DomGL Cloth, written up on Codrops in November 2026. His version is cloth on WebGPU. This one is paper on WebGL2 with three.js. Without WebGL2 you get the sheets as a flat row you can swipe.

Each sheet is a grid of points, 15 across, held by a pin a little off center. There’s no gravity. The air pushes along the sheet’s normal, a weak spring pulls every point back toward the flat pose, and the distance constraints are solved outward from the pin, so the pinned middle stays put and the edges follow it.

Cloth and paper differ in one place: paper refuses to crease. It bends broadly or not at all. Two changes get you there. The bend compliance drops from cloth territory to 1e-7.1, and the bend links reach four points apart as well as two, so a fold has to span a quarter of the sheet before it can form. The first pass without the long links looked like a wet tea towel.

Keeping it in one piece

Nothing stops a sheet from folding through itself, so it never gets the chance. The pin’s speed is capped at 1150 px/s and its change in speed at 7000 px/s². Fling the strip hard and the sheet travels as a rigid card instead of turning inside out. No point may get farther from the pin than it was at rest, which kills the stretch that a fast drag would otherwise show.

When a sheet has been quiet for 0.3s, it irons itself flat on a quintic over 0.7 to 1.8s, longer the more it was bent, and then stops being computed. At rest the canvas doesn’t redraw.

Light that leaves the print alone

The shading is relative to the flat pose. A flat sheet renders exactly as printed, with no wash of light across it, and only the bends gain highlight or shade. The surface itself is a cubic B-spline through the points, evaluated in the vertex shader, so 15 columns read as a smooth curve.

Shadows are the same surfaces drawn again, flattened onto a ground plane at a quarter of the resolution and blurred. Each sheet is turned into the page, so one long edge sits lower than the other, and the ground goes under that low edge. There are two shadows. A contact shadow sits 14px under it and is only dark where the paper is close: it fades out over the first 150px of lift, so the low edge reads crisp and the raised edge doesn’t. An ambient one sits 80px under, wider and fainter, and only suggests the lift.

The first version had a single shadow at 55% black, and every poster looked like a cutout glued to the page. The second measured the ground from the middle of each sheet, so the low half sat below its own floor and threw a hard sliver back toward the light.

The register

The top corner keeps a register of all seventeen: number, award, who gave it, date. It’s tied to the strip, so moving one poster along moves the list one row, and the row for the poster in front comes up to full ink as the one before it fades back. The top and bottom rows dissolve instead of stopping at an edge.

An earlier pass set every name huge behind the strip instead. It looked great in a still and fought the posters the moment anything moved. Seven small rows in a corner say the same thing and leave the paper alone.

The moving parts

  • Paper — Tissue, Bond or Card: bend compliance 1e-5.8, 1e-7.1 and 1e-8.3, with air 11, 8 and 6 and a return spring of 26, 36 and 50.
  • Air — multiplies how hard the air pushes on the face of the sheet; at 0 the paper only follows its pin.
  • Flutter — the gust noise that rides on speed, so a moving sheet ripples and a parked one doesn’t.
  • Shade — how much the bends darken and lighten against the flat print.
  • Contact, Ambient, Soft — the two shadow layers’ strength and how far their blur spreads.
  • Depth — how far the strip leans into the page, so the near end grows and the far end recedes.
  • Snap — on release, the strip settles with the nearest poster centered, so the register always comes to rest on a whole row.
